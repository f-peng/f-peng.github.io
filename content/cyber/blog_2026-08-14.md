## Conclusion
Cette semaine met en lumière des menaces croissantes, notamment le ransomware **Gunra** et l’exploit **ShieldBreak**, qui soulignent l’urgence de renforcer les défenses contre les attaques ciblant les infrastructures critiques et les solutions de sécurité elles-mêmes. Les vulnérabilités non notées dans les outils largement déployés (Palo Alto, Joomla, Zimbra, etc.) rappellent l’importance de surveiller les mises à jour des fournisseurs. Les équipes de sécurité doivent prioriser les correctifs et les audits de configuration pour limiter les risques d’exploitation.

<h3 class="class_h3">More From Our Main Blog: The Good, the Bad and the Ugly in Cybersecurity – Week 33</h3>
- 📅 Fri, 14 Aug 2026 15:21:37 +0000
- 🔗 Source: https://www.sentinelone.com/blog/the-good-the-bad-and-the-ugly-in-cybersecurity-week-33-8/

<h2>The Good | Courts Sentence « The Com » Online Syndicate Member for Blackmail &amp; Sextortion</h2>
<p>A court in the UK has <a href="https://www.nationalcrimeagency.gov.uk/news/com-group-member-sentenced-for-campaign-of-abuse-against-117-victims-worldwide" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">sentenced</a> a member of the decentralized online cybercrime collective known as “The Com” to two years in prison following an investigation by the National Crime Agency (NCA). <span class="marker-animation">Justin Swaddle, who operated under the digital aliases &lsquo;Epstein&rsquo;, &lsquo;Rugen&rsquo;, and &lsquo;Moscow&rsquo; across Discord, Snapchat, and Telegram, pleaded guilty to multiple criminal charges of blackmail and child abuse</span>. In addition to his sentence, the court ordered Swaddle&rsquo;s placement on the National Sex Offenders Register and imposed a ten-year Sexual Harm Prevention Order.</p>
<p>Investigators revealed that Swaddle systematically targeted and groomed young, vulnerable victims globally, using popular chat platforms to exploit his targets. The prosecution identified 117 female victims worldwide, aged thirteen to seventeen, whom Swaddle <a href="https://www.sentinelone.com/cybersecurity-101/threat-intelligence/what-is-social-engineering/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">coerced</a> into performing severe acts of self-harm and generating explicit material. Rather than seeking financial gain, <span class="marker-animation">Swaddle was reportedly motivated by the online status and notoriety he obtained by sharing the media within exclusive subgroups</span>. When victims resisted his demands, he used video recordings, <a href="https://www.sentinelone.com/cybersecurity-101/cybersecurity/what-is-personally-identifiable-information-pii-personal-health-information-phi/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">home addresses</a>, and school details to <a href="https://www.sentinelone.com/cybersecurity-101/threat-intelligence/cyber-extortion/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">blackmail</a> them into compliance.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.sentinelone.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/justin-swaddle-thecom.jpg" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-141638" height="559" src="https://www.sentinelone.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/justin-swaddle-thecom.jpg" width="447" /></a></p>
<p>The investigation, which the NCA initiated in January 2024 following Swaddle&rsquo;s initial arrest by West Yorkshire Police, required extensive cross-border coordination. British officers collaborated closely with law enforcement agencies in the United States, Australia, Canada, Norway, and New Zealand to identify and safeguard affected children worldwide.</p>
<p>Authorities emphasize that The Com functions as a highly dangerous, loose-knit global network subdivided into specialized factions, including groups dedicated to physical violence, sexual coercion, financial extortion, and high-profile corporate <a href="https://www.sentinelone.com/cybersecurity-101/threat-intelligence/what-is-double-extortion/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">ransomware</a> operations.</p>
<h2>The Bad | Agencies Warn of Expanding Gunra Ransomware Operations Targeting Critical Infrastructure</h2>
<p><span class="marker-animation">U.S., U.K., and South Korean intelligence and law enforcement agencies have issued a joint cybersecurity</span> <a href="https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCISA/bulletins/4244745" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">advisory</a> <span class="marker-animation">warning global</span> <a href="https://www.sentinelone.com/blog/securing-the-nations-critical-infrastructure-action-plans-to-defend-against-cyber-attacks/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">critical infrastructure</a> <span class="marker-animation">organizations about escalating threats by Gunra ransomware</span>. First appearing in April 2025 as a variant specializing in double extortion, the group uses malware derived from leaked <a href="https://www.sentinelone.com/anthology/conti/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Conti</a> source code. Gunra targets public health, financial, and government sectors worldwide, with a heavy concentration of victims in Australia, East Asia, and Europe.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.sentinelone.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/gunra_ransomware.jpg" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-141637" height="1080" src="https://www.sentinelone.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/gunra_ransomware.jpg" width="1920" /></a></p>
<p>To establish initial access, operators exploit critical authentication vulnerabilities, specifically <a href="https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/cve-2024-55591" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">CVE-2024-55591</a> and <a href="https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2025-24472" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">CVE-2025-24472</a>, in FortiOS and FortiProxy software, alongside security flaws in VPN gateways. While campaigns initially focused on Windows environments, <span class="marker-animation">the threat actors expanded to cross-platform operations by introducing a Linux variant</span>. In January 2026, the group launched a formal <a href="https://www.sentinelone.com/cybersecurity-101/threat-intelligence/what-is-ransomware-as-a-service-raas/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Ransomware-as-a-Service</a> (RaaS) affiliate program under the brand “Golden Community”, actively recruiting penetration testers to serve as initial access brokers. Attackers deploy their payloads via <a href="https://www.sentinelone.com/cybersecurity-101/threat-intelligence/phishing-scams/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">phishing</a> and conduct ransom negotiations via WhatsApp.</p>
<p>Once inside a network, the actors utilize Impacket tools for credential dumping and <a href="https://www.sentinelone.com/cybersecurity-101/threat-intelligence/lateral-movement/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">lateral movement</a>. They execute malicious tasks during nighttime hours, exfiltrating stolen documents to cloud services and deleting critical backup and archived data across primary and recovery centers. The malware leverages advanced ciphers like Salsa20 or ChaCha20 to encrypt terabytes of data in a limited timeframe.</p>
<p><span class="marker-animation">Strong links have been</span> <a href="https://asec.ahnlab.com/en/93421/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">identified</a> <span class="marker-animation">between Gunra and North Korean state-backed threat actors</span>, observing overlapping infrastructure and techniques, such as the exploitation of zero-day flaws in certificate signing software. Despite its sophistication, a catastrophic <a href="https://intel.breakglass.tech/post/gunra-ransomware-s-linux-variant-has-a-fatal-flaw-time-seeded-rand-makes-encrypted-files-recoverable-without-paying#recovery-procedure-for-incident-responders" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">cryptographic flaw</a> in Gunra&rsquo;s Linux variant allows victims to fully recover encrypted files.</p>
<h2>The Ugly | New ‘ShieldBreak’ Zero-Day Exploit Bypasses Microsoft Defender Protections</h2>
<p>A security researcher known as <span class="marker-animation">‘Nightmare Eclipse’ has released a novel Microsoft Defender zero-day exploit dubbed</span> ‘<a href="https://blog.projectnightcrawler.dev/posts/2026-08-11-shieldbreak-august-2026-disclosure/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">ShieldBreak</a>’ <span class="marker-animation">shortly after this month’s Patch Tuesday</span> <a href="https://msrc.microsoft.com/update-guide/releaseNote/2026-Aug" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">update</a>. The vulnerability operates as a direct patch bypass for <a href="https://msrc.microsoft.com/update-guide/en-US/advisory/CVE-2026-50656" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">RoguePlanet</a>, a separate privilege escalation flaw in Microsoft’s malware protection engine that was patched in July.</p>
<figure class="wp-caption alignnone" id="attachment_141636" style="width: 1196px;"><a href="https://www.sentinelone.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/shieldbreak_POC_exploit_demo.jpg" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="size-full wp-image-141636 magnify" height="1037" src="https://www.sentinelone.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/shieldbreak_POC_exploit_demo.jpg" width="1196" /></a><figcaption class="wp-caption-text" id="caption-attachment-141636">ShieldBreak PoC exploit demo (Source: Nightmare Eclipse)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Although both flaws lead to SYSTEM-level compromise, researchers <a href="https://cyberplace.social/@GossiTheDog/117082629057300721" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">confirm</a> the underlying exploitation techniques differ significantly. While the original RoguePlanet bug exploits a filesystem race condition using virtual disks to overwrite system files, <span class="marker-animation">ShieldBreak hijacks cloud-hydration processes</span>.</p>
<p>Specifically, the exploit leverages user-mode callback hooks to modify file contents during a cloud-hydration scan via the Cloud Filter API. To achieve privilege escalation, an attacker first places a standard test file and utilizes Object Manager symbolic links to redirect Defender&rsquo;s path to the system32 directory. During scanning, the exploit uses the Common Log File System to swap the file identity and plant a malicious DLL, <code>phoneinfo.dll</code>, where a default system file does not exist. Triggering a scheduled Windows Error Reporting task subsequently forces the system to load this rogue library, spawning a shell with highest privileges.</p>
<p>The proof-of-concept operates with a 100% success rate on fully <a href="https://www.sentinelone.com/cybersecurity-101/cybersecurity/what-is-patch-management/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">patched</a> installations of Windows 11 25H2 and Windows Server 2025. Although Windows 10 remains vulnerable to the flaw, the current code does not natively support those legacy systems. <span class="marker-animation">Analysts</span> <a href="https://infosec.exchange/@wdormann/117079587486018149" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">note</a> <span class="marker-animation">that Microsoft Defender must be actively enabled for the exploit chain to function</span>.</p>
<p>The release intensifies an ongoing <a href="https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/msrc/blog/2026/05/a-shared-responsibility-protecting-customers-through-coordinated-vulnerability-disclosure" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">dispute</a> between Microsoft and the researcher over bug bounty policies and recent <a href="https://x.com/msftsecresponse/status/2061293718942908925" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">threats</a> of legal action.</p>
